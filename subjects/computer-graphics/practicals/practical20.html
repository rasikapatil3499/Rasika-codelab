3D Transformation, Lighting & Texture

Transform 3D objects and study how lighting, materials and textures change the appearance of a rendered scene.

3D Transformations Lighting Materials Texture Mapping
01

Aim & Objectives

Aim: To apply 3D transformations and understand the visual effects of lighting, materials and texture mapping in a 3D graphics scene.

  • Apply translation, scaling and rotation to 3D objects.
  • Understand homogeneous coordinates and 4×4 transformation matrices.
  • Understand ambient, directional and point lighting.
  • Study material properties such as roughness and metalness.
  • Understand texture coordinates and texture mapping.
  • Compare the appearance of an object under different rendering conditions.

3D Transformation Theory

Translation

Translation changes the position of an object without changing its shape or orientation.

x′ = x + tx

y′ = y + ty

z′ = z + tz

Scaling

Scaling changes the dimensions of an object along the three axes.

y′ = syy

Lighting Fundamentals

Ambient Light

Provides general illumination to the scene. It does not strongly depend on a particular light direction.

Directional Light

Approximates light arriving from a particular direction, similar to sunlight at a large distance.

Point Light

Emits light from a specific position. Its direction relative to a surface changes across the scene.

Surface Appearance

Lighting interacts with material properties to produce highlights, shading and perceived depth.

Simple Lighting Model

I = Iambient + Idiffuse + Ispecular

The exact rendering equation used by a real-time graphics engine can be much more complex. The expression above is a useful conceptual model for understanding the major lighting components.

05

Texture Mapping

Texture mapping places a 2D image or procedural pattern onto the surface of a 3D object. Each surface vertex can contain texture coordinates, commonly called UV coordinates.

Texture coordinate

(u, v) → location inside a 2D texture

U Coordinate

Usually represents horizontal movement across the texture.

V Coordinate

Usually represents vertical movement across the texture.

Texture Wrapping

Controls what happens when UV coordinates extend beyond the normal texture range.

Observe

  • Rotation changes the object's orientation but not its dimensions.

11

Common Mistakes

  • Using degrees directly in APIs that expect radians.
  • Applying transformations in an unintended order.
  • Using a lighting-dependent material without providing useful illumination.
  • Confusing a texture image with the geometry of the 3D object.
  • Ignoring UV coordinates when texture mapping requires controlled placement.
  • Forgetting to update the camera or renderer after changing the viewport.
  • Using very large or very small texture images without considering performance.
12

Viva Questions

What is a 3D transformation?

A mathematical operation that changes an object's position, size or orientation in three-dimensional space.

Why are 4×4 matrices used in 3D graphics?

Homogeneous 4×4 matrices provide a common representation for translation, rotation, scaling and combinations of transformations.

What is lighting in computer graphics?

Lighting models the interaction between light sources and surfaces to determine their visible appearance.

What is a texture?

A texture is image or procedural data mapped onto a surface to provide additional visual detail.

What are UV coordinates?

UV coordinates identify positions in a 2D texture that correspond to locations on a 3D surface.

What is the difference between geometry and material?

Geometry defines the object's shape, while material defines how its surface appears and responds to lighting.

What is roughness?

Roughness is a material property that influences how broadly or sharply reflected light appears on a surface.
