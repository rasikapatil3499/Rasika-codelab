Cohen-Sutherland Algorithm

Repeatedly classify endpoints and move an outside endpoint to the appropriate window boundary.

01

Calculate outcodes

Find the 4-bit region code for both endpoints.

02

Test acceptance

If both codes are 0000, accept the complete line.

03

Test rejection

If the bitwise AND of the codes is non-zero, reject the line.

04

Find intersection

Otherwise, select an outside endpoint and calculate its intersection with the relevant boundary.

05

Replace endpoint

Update the endpoint and calculate its new outcode.

06

Repeat

Continue until the segment is accepted or rejected.

10

Common Mistakes

Confusing AND and OR

Use AND for trivial rejection and OR for checking whether at least one endpoint is outside.

Wrong boundary

Select the boundary represented by the set bit in the selected endpoint's outcode.

Incorrect slope equation

Keep x and y terms consistent when calculating intersections.

Forgetting to recalculate

After replacing an endpoint, calculate its new outcode before the next test.

Viva Questions

Test your understanding before moving to polygon clipping.

What is line clipping?

It is the process of removing the portions of a line outside a specified clipping window.

What is an outcode?

An outcode is a 4-bit region code that describes an endpoint's position relative to the rectangular clipping window.

What does 0000 mean?

The endpoint lies inside the clipping window.

When is a line trivially accepted?

When both endpoint outcodes are 0000.

When is a line trivially rejected?

When the bitwise AND of both endpoint outcodes is non-zero.

Why is clipping useful in computer graphics?

It avoids drawing portions of objects that lie outside the visible region.

What happens after an intersection is found?

The outside endpoint is replaced by the intersection point and its outcode is recalculated.

Why is Cohen-Sutherland efficient for many lines?

Many lines can be accepted or rejected quickly without calculating intersections when their outcodes permit a trivial decision.
