PRACTICAL 01

Explore the Data

Learn how to move from a raw dataset to your first meaningful insights. In this practical, you will inspect real-world data, understand its structure, calculate basic statistics and create visualizations that answer practical questions.

Data Exploration Python Pandas Basic Statistics Visualization Real-World Data
01 — LEARNING OBJECTIVE

First understand the data

Before creating advanced dashboards or applying machine learning, an analyst must understand what the dataset contains and what questions it can answer.

What you will learn

You will work with real-world datasets and learn the first stage of an analytics workflow: understanding the data before making decisions from it.

01 Load a dataset
02 Identify rows and columns
03 Understand data types
04 Calculate basic statistics
05 Find patterns
06 Select suitable visualizations

Questions an analyst should ask

Q01

What is in the dataset?

Identify the number of records, variables and important fields.

Q02

What type of data is present?

Identify numerical, categorical, date and other relevant variables.

Q03

What are the important values?

Calculate minimum, maximum, mean, median and other basic statistics.

Q04

What patterns can we see?

Use charts and comparisons to discover trends and relationships.

Complete all three problems. Each problem uses a different real-world context but follows the same analytics workflow.

PROBLEM 01

Supermarket Sales

A supermarket wants to understand its sales performance and identify the products and categories generating the highest sales.

Dataset
Field Type Example
Product Text Laptop
Category Category Electronics
Quantity Number 4
Price Number 45000
Find out
  • Which category sells the most?
  • Which product generates maximum revenue?
  • Which payment method is most common?
  • Which day has the highest sales?

04 — THINK LIKE AN ANALYST

Don't just create charts. Explain them.

For every visualization, write at least one meaningful observation based on the data.

01

What is the highest value?

Identify the category, product, student group or period with the highest value.

02

What is the lowest value?

Identify areas that may require attention or improvement.

03

Is there a trend?

Determine whether values are increasing, decreasing or remaining relatively stable.

04

Is there a relationship?

Compare two numerical variables and determine whether a visible relationship exists.

05

What surprised you?

Identify an unexpected pattern or unusual observation in the dataset.

06

What decision can be made?

Convert your observation into a simple real-world recommendation.
