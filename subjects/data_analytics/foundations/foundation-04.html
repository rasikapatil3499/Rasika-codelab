02 — DESCRIPTIVE STATISTICS

Describe Your Data

A small set of statistics can quickly summarize a large collection of observations.

10 20 20 30 40 80
MEAN 33.3 Average value
MEDIAN 25 Middle value
MODE 20 Most frequent value
RANGE 70 Maximum − Minimum
THINK ABOUT THE DATA

Mean vs Median: Why Does It Matter?

A single extreme value can strongly affect the mean while the median may remain more stable.

₹20K ₹22K ₹24K ₹25K ₹28K ₹5L
MEAN Strongly affected by the extreme salary
MEDIAN More representative of the typical employee
Analyst thinking:

When data is skewed or contains extreme values, the median may provide a better representation of a typical observation.

03 — DISTRIBUTION

How Are Values Distributed?

Distribution tells us how observations are spread across different values.

0 20 40 60 80 100
What can you ask?

Where are most observations concentrated? Are the values spread out? Is there a long tail? Are there multiple groups?

04 — VARIABILITY

Average Isn't Everything

Two datasets can have the same average but very different levels of variability.

Dataset A

48 49 50 51 52
Mean = 50

Values are close together.

Dataset B

10 30 50 70 90
Mean = 50

Values are widely spread.

Important:

Mean alone does not describe a dataset. Range, variance, and standard deviation help us understand its spread.

06 — RELATIONSHIPS

Do Two Variables Move Together?

Correlation helps us investigate whether two numerical variables are related.

X Study Hours
↔
Y Exam Marks

If students who study more hours tend to obtain higher marks, the two variables may have a positive relationship.

Important:

Correlation indicates association. It does not automatically prove that one variable causes the other.

07 — TIME-BASED ANALYSIS

Find Trends

When data contains dates or time, we can investigate growth, decline, seasonality, and sudden changes.

Possible Insight

Sales show an overall upward trend over the six-month period.

04
